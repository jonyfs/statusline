# Title that states the claim, not the topic

## One subtitle line that tells the reader what they get

Open on the problem or the surprise. Two sentences, no scene-setting. The reader should
already know by the end of this paragraph what the article argues.

State the thesis. One sentence.

## A heading that says something

Show the thing first — code, a number, an error — then explain it.

```text
short, self-contained sample; no project identifiers; under ~25 lines
```

Explain what the sample means and why it mattered here.

## The part only someone who did this work could write

What was tried first, how it failed, what the failure taught. Keep the specifics: the number,
the message, the wrong assumption.

## What transfers to the reader's own work

The generalizable point, stated plainly, with its limits.

Close where the argument closes. What changed, or what comes next. No summary, no question
fishing for comments.
